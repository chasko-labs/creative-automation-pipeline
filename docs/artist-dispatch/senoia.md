# artist dispatch — Senoia, Georgia 30276 (Coweta farm country)

Market: US-SE-ATL metro (Publix Super Market at The Plaza Midtown anchor)
via frontier US-GA-SENOIA. Retailers: Publix, Walmart, Kroger.
Audience: Publix southeast porch families — Sunday porch stacks, kids and
cubs, Bear Bites in lunchboxes.
Subscription: the Kodiak Cakes subscription service (Subscribe & Save, 15%
off + free shipping over $45) — pantry boxes delivered to farm compounds
and rural routes outside town. Never a third-party CSA; farms below are
ingredient sources only.
Ingredient sources (family farms preferred): Thompson Produce (Senoia),
Durham's Produce (Newnan), 180 Degree Farm (Sharpsburg); peach halo Dickey
Farms (Musella), Pearson Farm (Fort Valley). Market anchor: Senoia Farmers
Market, Main Street, Saturdays.

## global frame (every plate prompt carries this)

- plating: round plate seen slightly from above, short stack of plain round
  flat cakes center plate, topping arranged over the stack, fork at the
  right side of the plate. No hand, no bite, no syrup pour.
- style tail: black ink only on white ground (frozen mono recipe).
- palette law (`docs/plate-dispatch.md`): every sensory word ships with its
  ink translation. Shape/size first, ingredient name last. Never write hue
  words (golden, red, green); write density, spacing, and line work.

## place constants (year-round Senoia)

- Piedmont brick main street: low two-story brick storefronts, deep awnings,
  parallel-parked pickups, live oaks over the sidewalk. Farm compounds out
  of town: white farmhouse, tin-roof barn, wire fencing, red clay drives,
  pine windbreaks.
- Climate: humid subtropical — haze and cicada hum in summer, soft gray
  mild winters, first frost roughly November, last frost roughly late March.
- Year-round birds: northern mockingbird, eastern bluebird, Carolina wren,
  red-bellied woodpecker, American goldfinch. Year-round plants: loblolly
  pine, southern magnolia, white oak, sweetgum, red clay earth.

## 1. deep winter (Jan–Feb) — frost mornings, woodsmoke, bare oaks

What the place looks like: low gray skies, bare white-oak and sweetgum
limbs over Main Street, pine the only green. Farm fields rest under rye
cover. Porch stacks eaten indoors; steam on windows. Red clay dark after
rain.
Animals present: white-throated sparrows scratching under feeders,
dark-eyed juncos in the rye, yellow-rumped warblers in the pines,
red-tailed hawk over fallow fields. Gray squirrels on the oaks.
Seasonal looks and touches: enamel mugs, flannel at the table, mason jar of
pecans carried over from fall, woodstove kettle. Porch light on at supper.

### 1a. collard-ribbon savory cakes (December–February, post-frost best)

Recipe: 1 cup Buttermilk Power Cakes mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg + 1 tbsp
melted butter; fold in 1/2 cup finely shredded post-frost collards
(Thompson Produce, Senoia — frost sweetens the leaf) wilted 2 minutes in
the butter with a pinch of salt and a sliver of Vidalia kept from spring
(frozen). Griddle 3 minutes per side. Top with a spoon of pot-liquor
reduction (collard cooking liquid simmered to a glaze) and crumbled
cornbread.
Dispatch line: broad flat leaves, cut in thin ribbons, overlapping 4-5
over the stack, leaves white with center rib lines and wide spacing, plus
scattered handful of coarse pale crumbs distinct around the ribbons.

### 1b. turnip-cube hash cakes (January–February)

Recipe: batter as 1a. Peel and 1-inch dice 1 cup turnips (Durham's Produce,
Newnan), griddle-crisp in butter until edges firm, fold half into the
batter, pile the rest over the stack with cracked pepper. Side of scrambled
egg optional.
Dispatch line: 1-inch firm cubes, edges worked with dense crosshatching,
faces left white, piled 6-8 over the stack, faces unbroken, no fill.

## 2. spring + Easter (Mar–May) — dogwood, azalea, strawberry tables

What the place looks like: dogwood white bloom and azalea drifts along
Main Street, pine pollen dusting everything pale yellow-green, fields
turned and planted. Senoia Farmers Market reopens Saturdays; strawberry
tables sell out by ten. Easter morning: church, then porch brunch.
Animals present: barn swallows back under barn eaves, eastern kingbirds on
fence wire, ruby-throated hummingbirds at feeders, eastern cottontails at
field edges. Chorus frogs in the low ditches.
Seasonal looks and touches: market tote of strawberries on the passenger
seat, wide-brim hat, jelly jar of sweet tea, Easter table with a folded
cloth napkin beside the plate. Wisteria over the porch rail.
Plants: flowering dogwood, azalea, redbud, wisteria, wild blackberry bloom
at fencerows.

### 2a. spring strawberry wedge cakes — Easter card (March–May)

Recipe: 1 cup Power Cakes mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg + 1 tsp lemon zest
(imported flavor tradition, noted). Hull and quarter 1 cup strawberries
(Senoia market tables, Thompson Produce); macerate 10 minutes with 1 tsp
sugar. Stack three cakes, spoon berries and their pooled juice over the top
— still pool, never mid-pour. Easter build adds a dollop of whipped cream.
Dispatch line: halves and wedges showing cut faces, 2-3 pieces plus
quartered wedges, cut faces left white with seed-dot stipple, arranged over
the stack with one small white highlight gap per piece; Easter build adds
one round white dollop, smooth outline, center plate.

### 2b. Vidalia onion savory cakes (April–May, early leaves April)

Recipe: batter as 1a, fold in 1/2 cup thin-sliced Vidalia onion (early
season April from the Vidalia belt via Durham's, peak May) caramelized low
and slow — render as soft translucent arcs, not color. Top with a fried egg
and cracked pepper. This is the savory porch-supper plate.
Dispatch line: long thin arcs, fanned 3-4 in parallel over the stack, arcs
drawn with fine parallel line shading along the lower edge, faces left
white; one round egg white with clean unbroken outline beside the stack.

## 3. early summer (Jun) — blueberries, crepe myrtle, first corn

What the place looks like: crepe myrtle blooming along Main Street,
humidity rising, afternoon thunderheads. Blueberry buckets at the market;
first sweet corn. Porch fans on. Fireflies by mid-June.
Animals present: mockingbirds singing past midnight, bluebirds working the
fence posts, box turtles crossing clay drives, hummingbirds constant.
Seasonal looks and touches: enamel pie pan of blueberries on the counter,
mason jars, checked cloth, market crate as photo prop. Kids barefoot with
berry-stained fingers — hands out of frame at plating.

### 3a. blueberry burst cakes (June)

Recipe: 1 cup Power Cakes mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg; fold 3/4 cup
blueberries (180 Degree Farm, Sharpsburg, or Durham's) into the batter so
some burst on the griddle. Top with 1/4 cup fresh berries and a still pool
of warmed blueberry compote in a small pitcher alongside.
Dispatch line: pea-sized round berries, scattered 5-7 over the stack plus
burst berries shown as broken rings with stippled centers, each with one
small white highlight gap; small pitcher alongside, still pool inside,
never mid-pour.

## 4. high summer + July 4th (Jul) — Coweta peach peak, butterbeans

What the place looks like: full humid subtropical July — hazy mornings,
cicada roar by noon, thunderheads by four. Peach stands stacked high along
Highway 16; butterbeans shelled on porches. July 4th: Main Street parade,
bunting on the brick storefronts, fireworks over the farm fields.
Animals present: barn swallows swooping the fields, eastern kingbirds,
cicadas (annual + brood hum), bobwhite quail calling from fencerows at
dawn, cottontails in the beans.
Seasonal looks and touches: July 4th build — small cloth bunting on the
porch rail behind the plate, never on the plate. Paper flat of peaches on
the counter, shelling bowl of butterbeans, iced tea sweating. Subscription
box open on the porch with the Family Size bag — rural-route delivery cue.

### 4a. Coweta peach wedge cakes — July 4th card (May–Aug, peak July)

Recipe: 1 cup Power Cakes mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg + pinch cinnamon. Peel
and wedge 1 ripe Coweta peach (Thompson Produce or the peach halo: Dickey
Farms, Pearson Farm); griddle wedges 1 minute per side in butter until
edges firm. Stack, fan wedges over the top, finish with a still pool of
peach syrup in a small pitcher alongside.
Dispatch line: halves and wedges showing cut faces, 2-3 wedges fanned over
the stack, cut faces left white with pit-cavity curve line, edges worked
with dense crosshatching, one small white highlight gap per wedge.

### 4b. butterbean savory cakes (May–Aug)

Recipe: batter as 1a. Simmer 1 cup shelled butterbeans (Senoia market)
tender with salt pork removed before serving — beans only on the plate.
Spoon beans over the stack with a knob of butter melting still (drawn as a
small white square with soft outline, no drip). Cracked pepper.
Dispatch line: small loose ovals, scattered handful of 8-10, individual
pieces distinct, each with a short center crease line, piled over the
stack; one small white square melting still at the center.

## 5. late summer (Aug) — watermelon stands, muscadines turning

What the place looks like: heat holding, fields tall, watermelon trucks at
the crossroads. Muscadine vines on farm fences turning from hard green to
bronze and dark. Goldenrod starting at the road shoulders. School starts;
lunchboxes need Bear Bites.
Animals present: goldfinches on the coneflowers, hummingbirds tanking up
before migration, katydids at night, rat snakes in the barn (respectful
distance, never on the plate).
Seasonal looks and touches: watermelon rind curls in a tub, market crate
stenciled with the farm name, lunchbox open beside the plate for the Bear
Bites back-to-school build.

### 5a. watermelon wedge cakes (August)

Recipe: plain sweet stack (1 cup mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg + 1 tbsp
sugar). Cut 2 small triangular wedges of watermelon (Durham's), rind
removed, chilled. Lay wedges against the stack, scatter 5-7 seeds as small
dark teardrops. Serve with a still glass of tea.
Dispatch line: two triangular wedges leaning on the stack, cut faces left
white with fine radiating line texture, rind edge as a firm hatched band;
5-7 small teardrop seeds scattered, filled solid black.

### 5b. muscadine compote cakes (late August–September)

Recipe: 2 cups muscadines (Coweta farm fences and stands, peak September),
slip skins, simmer pulp 10 minutes, strain seeds, return skins to simmer to
a thick compote. Spoon over the stack; skins render as thick curls.
Dispatch line: small round fruit, skins as thick curled cups, scattered
5-7, cups drawn with dense crosshatched edges and white centers, pooled
compote as a still white ring with stippled edge around the stack base.

## 6. fall harvest + Halloween (Sep–Oct) — pecans dropping, goldenrod

What the place looks like: goldenrod and late black-eyed Susan on the
shoulders, sweetgum turning, pecans dropping on tin roofs. Senoia Farmers
Market harvest tables; Halloween pumpkins on Main Street doorsteps, hay
bales by the brick storefronts.
Animals present: white-tailed deer at field edges at dusk, wild turkey
flocks in the pines, migrating hawks overhead, gray squirrels cutting
hickory nuts.
Seasonal looks and touches: Halloween build — one small whole pumpkin
beside the plate (never carved on the plate), hay-bale texture under the
table setting. Pecan-gathering bucket, flannel back on.

### 6a. pecan praline drizzle cakes — Halloween card (October–November)

Recipe: 1 cup Power Cakes mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg. Toast 1/2 cup pecan
halves (Coweta groves via Thompson Produce) in butter; simmer 1/4 cup brown
sugar + 2 tbsp cream to a still pourable praline; pour tableside into a
small pitcher, never mid-pour. Pile pecans over the stack, pitcher
alongside.
Dispatch line: pecan halves as ridged ovals, piled 6-8, each with center
crease and crosshatched shell-side texture, faces left white; small pitcher
alongside with still pool inside.

## 7. Thanksgiving (Nov) — sweet potatoes, pecan pie_tables, frost

What the place looks like: first frost silvering the rye, sweetgum bare,
smoke from burn piles. Thanksgiving table: the good cloth, the stack as
the morning-after breakfast using leftover pie nuts and storage sweets.
Animals present: wild turkeys bold in the fields, deer in the bottoms,
winter wrens in the brush piles, red-tailed hawks on the bare limbs.
Seasonal looks and touches: pie-safe open on the counter, cloth napkin,
whole sweet potatoes in a crate, pecan pie cooling — morning-after stack
wears the leftovers.

### 7a. sweet potato mash cakes — Thanksgiving card (November)

Recipe: 1 cup Power Cakes mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg + 1/2 cup roasted
mashed sweet potato (storage sweets, Durham's) + 1 tsp pie spice (imported
tradition, noted). Stack high; top with toasted pecans and a still pool of
maple in the side pitcher.
Dispatch line: short stack built visibly taller (four cakes), faces left
white with stippled crumb texture; 1-inch firm cubes of roasted sweet
potato worked with dense crosshatched edges piled 5-6 over the top, plus
ridged pecan ovals scattered 4-5.

## 8. winter holidays + Christmas (Dec) — porch lights, collards sweet

What the place looks like: Main Street strung with lights on the brick
faces, wreaths on the storefronts, mild gray days with a frost snap after
the front passes. Collards at their sweetest. Christmas morning stack
before the presents.
Animals present: goldfinches at the thistle sock, Carolina wrens in the
wreaths, white-throated sparrows under the feeder, opossum prints in the
frost (tracks only, respectful distance).
Seasonal looks and touches: Christmas build — sprig of magnolia with a red
bow behind the plate (never on it), porch lights bokeh behind, flannel,
enamel mugs. Subscription gift-box cue: the Kodiak Cakes box with tissue,
rural-route porch delivery.
Plants: southern magnolia foliage, longleaf pine boughs, red holly berries
at the woods edge (rendered as pea-sized rounds, no hue words).

### 8a. Christmas morning pecan-honey cakes (December)

Recipe: plain stack (1 cup mix + 2/3 cup milk + 1 egg + 1 tbsp melted
butter). Warm 1/3 cup wildflower honey (180 Degree Farm) with 2 tbsp butter
to a still pool; pour tableside, pitcher alongside. Pile toasted pecans
6-8 over the stack. Magnolia sprig behind the plate only.
Dispatch line: ridged pecan ovals piled 6-8 over the stack, center creases,
crosshatched shell sides, faces left white; small pitcher alongside with
still pool, one white highlight gap on the pool surface.

### 8b. New Year collard and pot-liquor cakes (late December)

Recipe: repeat 1a with the season's sweetest post-frost collards;
add a spoon of black-eyed peas (small loose ovals with center crease dots)
for the New Year table. Cornbread crumble.
Dispatch line: thin ribbon leaves overlapping 4-5 with rib lines, plus
scattered handful of small ovals each with a single center dot, pieces
distinct, all white with line work only.
